Interface is the policy
Default activation, consent friction, and notification timing are not UX choices. They are the governance architecture — expressed through product settings instead of legal language.
Design = governance
🏢
The blind spot is external
Enterprise AI governance is internally focused — internal copilots, internal data, internal risk registers. Real exposure arrives via public platforms where employees post content that feeds AI remix engines by default.
External surfaces unmapped
⏱
Product cycles beat governance
Platforms deploy AI features before awareness forms, before consent frameworks exist, and before regulators respond. By the time governance catches up, participation is already normalised and reversal is commercially painful.
Timing asymmetry
Governance intent becomes visible where resistance requires more effort than participation.
The platforms deploying AI most aggressively reveal their governance intent not through policy documents — but through which actions they make frictionless and which they make expensive. For enterprise leaders: ask platforms to show their default map for your content. Treat interface defaults as governance decisions, not design details.
